qbeam moves a file from a computer screen to your phone using a stream of QR codes. Run one command on the computer, point your phone at the screen, and the file arrives, checked byte for byte.

No network connection between the two devices, no USB cable, no Bluetooth pairing and no account. The computer only needs to show a web page or a terminal window.

HOW IT WORKS
1. On the computer, run: uvx qbeam send report.pdf (or npx qbeam send report.pdf). Nothing needs admin rights to install.
2. Open qbeam on your phone and point the camera at the codes.
3. The file is saved to Downloads/qbeam once it's complete and its SHA-256 checksum matches.

WHERE IT HELPS
• Cloud shells, remote desktops and virtual machines that can show a screen but can't easily share a file with your phone
• Lab machines, kiosks and computers without your phone's cable or apps
• Getting a log, a config file or a build artifact onto your phone in seconds

FAST
• Up to ~280 KB/s measured on a OnePlus 12 with the sender's "max" setting: a 2 MB photo in about 8 seconds. Text and logs are compressed first, so they go faster
• A grid of QR codes read in parallel on the phone, at up to 60 frames a second where the camera allows
• A fountain code: a missed frame never means waiting for the loop to come round again

FOR DEVELOPERS
• Terminal mode: over SSH or without a browser, the codes are drawn right in the terminal
• Send a folder as one archive (respects .gitignore), or pipe in stdin
• Optional encryption: AES-256-GCM with a passphrase you type on the phone

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• The app doesn't request internet access. Nothing you receive leaves your phone
• No account, no analytics, no ads
• Open source (Apache-2.0): github.com/qbeam/qbeam

Anyone who can see the sending screen can read an unencrypted transfer. Use encryption for anything sensitive, and only move data you're authorised to move.

Privacy policy: https://qbeam.dev/privacy/
