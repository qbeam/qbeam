qbeam moves a file from a computer screen to your iPhone using a stream of QR codes. Run one command on the computer, point your iPhone at the screen, and the file arrives, checked byte for byte.

No network connection between the two devices, no cable, no AirDrop and no account. The computer only needs to show a web page or a terminal window, so it works with Windows and Linux machines too.

HOW IT WORKS
1. On the computer, run: uvx qbeam send report.pdf (or npx qbeam send report.pdf). Nothing needs admin rights to install.
2. Open qbeam on your iPhone and point the camera at the codes.
3. The file is saved in the Files app (On My iPhone › qbeam) once it's complete and its SHA-256 checksum matches. Share it to any app from there.

WHERE IT HELPS
• Cloud shells, remote desktops and virtual machines that can show a screen but can't easily share a file with your phone
• Lab machines, kiosks and shared computers
• Getting a log, a config file or a build artifact onto your iPhone in seconds

FAST
• Up to ~280 KB/s measured on iPhone 15 with the sender's "max" setting: a 2 MB photo in about 8 seconds. Text and logs are compressed first, so they go faster
• The camera runs at 60 frames a second and a grid of QR codes is read in parallel
• A fountain code: a missed frame never means waiting for the loop to come round again

FOR DEVELOPERS
• Terminal mode: over SSH or without a browser, the codes are drawn right in the terminal
• Send a folder as one archive (respects .gitignore), or pipe in stdin
• Optional encryption: AES-256-GCM with a passphrase you type on the iPhone

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• The app makes no network connections. Nothing you receive leaves your iPhone
• No account, no analytics, no ads
• Open source (Apache-2.0): github.com/qbeam/qbeam

Anyone who can see the sending screen can read an unencrypted transfer. Use encryption for anything sensitive, and only move data you're authorised to move.
